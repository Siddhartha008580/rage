Maximum concentration of semaglutide is<br>reached 1 to 3 days post dose.<br>Similar exposure is achieved with subcutaneous administration of semaglutide in the abdomen,<br>thigh, or upper arm.<br>In patients with type 2 diabetes, semaglutide exposure increases in a dose-proportional manner<br>for once-weekly doses of 0.5 mg, 1 mg and 2 mg. Semaglutide is extensively bound to<br>plasma albumin (>99%).<br>Elimination<br>The apparent clearance of semaglutide in patients with type 2 diabetes is approximately 0.05 L/h.<br>With an elimination half-life of approximately 1 week, semaglutide will be present in the circulation<br>for about 5 weeks after the last dose.<br>_Metabolism_<br>The primary route of elimination for semaglutide is metabolism following proteolytic cleavage of<br>the peptide backbone and sequential beta-oxidation of the fatty acid sidechain.<br>_Excretion_<br>The primary excretion routes of semaglutide-related material are via the urine and feces.<br>Approximately 3% of the dose is excreted in the urine as intact semaglutide.<br>Specific Populations<br>Based on a population pharmacokinetic analysis, age, sex, race, and ethnicity, and renal<br>impairment do not have a clinically meaningful effect on the pharmacokinetics of semaglutide.<br>The exposure of semaglutide decreases with an increase in body weight. However, semaglutide<br>doses of 0.5 mg and 1 mg provide adequate systemic exposure over the body weight range of 40<br>to 198 kg evaluated in the clinical trials.
